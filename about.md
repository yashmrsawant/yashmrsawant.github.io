  <section class="cv-section" id="research">
    <div class="cv-section-header">
      <span class="cv-section-kicker">Doctoral Research · Indian Institute of Science</span>
      <h2 class="cv-section-title">Selected Research Projects</h2>
    </div>

    <div class="research-projects-stack">
      <div class="research-project-card">
        <div class="cv-card-top">
          <div>
            <h3 class="project-title">Circuit mechanisms of visual change detection in the midbrain</h3>
            <div class="project-subtitle">Biologically inspired recurrent neural networks</div>
          </div>
          <span class="cv-year-badge">2019 – 2022</span>
        </div>
        <ul class="project-bullets">
          <li>Designed a sparse, topographic recurrent neural network inspired by the superior colliculus/optic tectum (SC/OT) and associated inhibitory circuitry to detect changes in natural scenes.</li>
          <li>Achieved <strong>ninefold faster learning</strong> with <strong>15-fold fewer connections</strong> than a conventional RNN. Low-dimensional model dynamics suggested distinct populations for persistent stimulus representations and change detection.</li>
          <li>Generated testable predictions that populations supporting stimulus maintenance and change detection differ in recurrent connectivity, including sparsity and the spatial extent of their connections.</li>
          <li>Reproduced reported neural and behavioral effects, including stimulus competition and microstimulation effects, and predicted human gaze fixations during change blindness.</li>
        </ul>
        <div class="pub-links" style="margin-top: 0.75rem;">
          <a href="https://github.com/yashmrsawant/ChangeDetection_stRNN" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pub-link-pill">Repository: github.com/yashmrsawant/ChangeDetection_stRNN ↗</a>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="research-project-card">
        <div class="cv-card-top">
          <div>
            <h3 class="project-title">Latent brain dynamics and individual differences in cognition</h3>
            <div class="project-subtitle">Gaussian process latent variable models of fMRI</div>
          </div>
          <span class="cv-year-badge">2022 – 2026</span>
        </div>
        <ul class="project-bullets">
          <li>Developed Gaussian process latent variable models of large-scale fMRI dynamics using data from the Human Connectome Project (HCP; a dataset of 1,000+ participants).</li>
          <li>Selected latent dimensionality through cross-validation, <strong>reducing data dimensionality by more than fivefold</strong> and identifying networks with distinct slow and fast dynamics.</li>
          <li>Modeled temporal structure through timescale parameters in the latent Gaussian processes, linking spatial network organization to the temporal properties of brain activity.</li>
          <li>Identified associations between fluid intelligence and the dynamics of a model-derived network topologically similar to the canonical frontoparietal network.</li>
          <li>Found task-dependent associations: higher fluid intelligence was associated with relatively passive network dynamics during rest or low-demand tasks and relatively active dynamics during high-demand tasks.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <div class="research-project-card">
        <div class="cv-card-top">
          <div>
            <h3 class="project-title">Hemodynamic observation models for latent neural dynamics</h3>
            <div class="project-subtitle">Gaussian process variational autoencoder</div>
          </div>
          <span class="cv-year-badge">In Preparation</span>
        </div>
        <ul class="project-bullets">
          <li>Developed a Gaussian process variational autoencoder (GP-VAE) with an explicit hemodynamic observation model for fMRI blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) responses.</li>
          <li>Formulated the mapping from latent neural activity to measured BOLD signals as a <strong>linear time-invariant (LTI) system</strong>, accounting for temporal filtering introduced by hemodynamics.</li>
          <li>Designed the model to distinguish latent neural dynamics from the hemodynamic measurement process, addressing a fundamental source of confounding in the interpretation of fMRI timescales.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
